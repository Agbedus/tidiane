About

Architect of Africa's
Space Sovereignty

H.E. Dr. Tidiane Ouattara is the elected President of the Council of the African Space Agency. A space scientist with a PhD in Remote Sensing and GIS from Université de Sherbrooke (Canada), he has built a 29-year career at the intersection of space science, policy, and international diplomacy — holding senior leadership roles at the Canadian Space Agency, Natural Resources Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada, and the African Union Commission.

He has mobilised over US $155M for African space and Earth observation programmes, led the GMES & Africa initiative across 45 countries, and driven the ratification of the African Space Policy and Strategy by all 55 AU member states. His work bridges science and diplomacy, forging international partnerships that translate space knowledge into agricultural resilience, disaster preparedness, and climate action for African communities.

"Space is not a luxury for Africa. It is infrastructure — as essential as roads, as vital as water."

Career Highlights

A Lifetime of
Strategic Leadership

2024—
President, Council of the African Space Agency
African Space Agency (AfSA), Cairo, Egypt
Elected by the African Union Assembly as the inaugural President of the AfSA Council. Leading the operationalisation of the Agency — including the build-out of its headquarters, the recruitment of 156 permanent staff, and the negotiation of strategic partnerships with ESA, NASA, JAXA, ISRO, and CNSA.
2016–24
Manager, African Space Programme
African Union Commission, Addis Ababa
Led the development and implementation of the African Outer Space Programme and the GMES & Africa Programme (€55M). Drove the ratification of the African Space Policy and Strategy by all 55 member states. Mobilised over €100M in investment, including the Africa–Europe Space Partnership Programme.
2010–16
Head Manager — Geospatial Programs
Environment and Climate Change Canada
Led geospatial data management and ecosystem assessment planning for the Canadian Wildlife Service. Developed the geospatial component of Canada's National Conservation Plan. Managed teams of 20 staff and a budget of over CAD $3M.
2004–06
International Relations Manager
Canadian Space Agency
Managed international space policy portfolios covering Earth Observation, Navigation, Robotics, and Space Sciences for Africa, the Americas, and the United Nations. Led the Canadian delegation to UNCOPUOS.
1996–01
PhD in Remote Sensing & GIS / Lecturer
Université de Sherbrooke, Canada
Earned a PhD in Remote Sensing and Geographical Information Systems. Taught courses in hydrology, satellite image analysis, GIS, and climatology. Published peer-reviewed research on erosion modelling in semi-arid environments.
